# Persona programs — a companion's traits driving the device

A companion's chat diverges by persona and their program does not: the
Companions engine generates the same shape whoever is talking. Mapping their
traits onto Groove's knobs — `intensity` to the speed-percent magnitude,
`variety` to the timing and dip variability — is what would make a program
theirs rather than a default one.

Which traits are code at all comes first. A trait earns a manifest field and a
mapping only if code reads it; one that colours how a companion behaves belongs
in the persona prompt, where an author can already write it:

- `chattiness` and `playfulness` are code, and shipped with ambient chat because
  they drive a timer;
- `dominance` is how readily a companion overrides what you asked for, which the
  prompt can carry on its own;
- `variety` may be the same, or may be the knob above.

Settling that before adding fields matters because a manifest field is a
compatibility surface, and packs in the wild make one expensive to take back.

The payoff is the thing to prove: that character bends both the chat and the
generated program. The chooser and the further companions have shipped, and
their contrast is prompt-only until their programs diverge by trait.
